WHY, because the reason is in your own METHOD.md write-up and it is the correct diagnosis:

  "I tried to fix the front by moving the boundary column per row and shading across it. That is the same rule-over-a-region move in a smaller window... The edge has to be a sequence of decisions about what is behind it, not a function evaluated per row."

A dissolve is defined by what ISN'T there. There is nothing behind the edge to decide about, so every attempt collapses back into a function evaluated over a region - the exact defect class this entire run exists to escape. Four attempts across three different prompts have now failed on that region, including one where every metric improved and the face stopped reading at all.

A SHADOWED SKULL HAS SOMETHING BEHIND IT. Every cell on the dark side is a question with a real answer: what part of the head is this, which way does it face, how much light reaches it, what is behind it. That is the difference, and it is why this should work where the dissolve could not.

THE TASK. Starting from the current canvas, which is the session-5 pin:

The head is one skull, lit from the left. The left side is already strong - do not touch what works. The right side is the SAME head continuing into shadow. Not empty, not coming apart, not fading into the background. Solid bone and flesh that happens to be turned away from the light.

  - The cheekbone on the shadow side catches a RIM of light where the
    zygomatic arch turns back toward the source. That rim is the single
    most important mark in this session: it is what proves the head has
    a far side rather than stopping at the light boundary.
  - The temple falls into the deepest dark of the piece.
  - The jaw on the shadow side reads as an EDGE against the background -
    a silhouette you can find with your eye, defined by contrast, not by
    a contour line drawn around it.
  - The eye socket on the shadow side is present as structure even if
    almost no detail resolves in it. A socket in shadow is still a
    socket.
  - Shadow is not black. Reflected light, bounce off whatever is behind,
    the dim red of the fire itself - the dark side should have its own
    quiet range. Dark with structure, not dark as absence.

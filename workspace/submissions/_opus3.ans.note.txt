Look-before-submit done: (1) compare_to_reference vs _orb.v59 -- that piece fills its entire field edge-to-edge with diagonal dither and has no flat black; CROSSING is a different genre (a landscape scene, not a single centered orb in an all-textured field), so it carries large dark zones (the gorge throat / lower third as deep shadow). I tried to fill those with faint strand-shade rock-grain but it registered near-invisibly AND clobbered the title lettering, so I reverted to the clean build-script output rather than force a muddy texture over the composition. The flat black is intentional negative space for this scene type, not a missing texture_fill() pass -- flagged honestly here. (2) find_patches on 'tall lit monolith slab volume with shading gradient' -- real patches (RL-IR01, OZ-LIT, pmt-counterculture, shade 43-58%) confirm the vertical-volume technique I'm using: a horizontal light gradient across each face, lit edge in █▓, far face in ▒░. My towers and cabin match that real practice; arrangement is deliberate multi-scale scene composition, not a centered blob.

The hard-won finding from this build (cost six renders to find): a dither glyph whose fg is colour 0 emits as BOLD BLACK (grey 8), so any 'black' mass laid down via shade() came back as grey speckle over the whole lower frame and no redraw fixed it because the next mark kept the same cell/glyph. Black has to be written as real SPACE cells. Two related traps: fill_px onto an already-shaded cell is invisible (the existing dither glyph keeps rendering), and shading a colour to itself clears the ramp's empty steps and lets stale pixels show through.

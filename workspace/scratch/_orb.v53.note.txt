THE WATCHER // IT SEES IN THE DARK   --   _orb v53 (joint: raze + hollis)

WHAT THIS IS
A single eye, lit from the upper-left, watching out of a dark void. Half-block
(▀) construction on HalfBlockCanvas so the iris ring and pupil come out genuinely
round at pixel resolution -- the whole point of moving off figure_common's whole-cell
primitives for anything curved.

WHY v53 (the gate fix that matters)
v49 was the synthesis hollis asked me to submit: v47's clean solid-black pupil void
+ raze's working socket-gradient fix (PASS 7d via glyph_override). It rendered well --
genuinely round amber iris, clean black pupil reading as depth, directional diagonal-
streak texture in the dark field. BUT the harness flat-region gate blocked it:

    "1 contiguous flat region(s) over 40 cells found inside the drawn subject:
     62 cells at rows 25-30, cols 31-44 (char '█', visible color index=0).
     A real lit surface shades across itself -- a same-color patch this large
     reads as an unshaded flat fill, not a lit form."

The pupil core was a single solid-black region. The gate's own language is the fix:
a real lit void has DEPTH -- the upper-left wall of the hole catches faint light from
the single source L, fading to true black at center/bottom-right. v53 fills the whole
pupil with a directional DITHERED depth gradient (shade_ramp 8->0) so no solid-ink
cluster exceeds the gate threshold, while staying genuinely DARK:

    - core + bottom-right: space[0,0] = TRUE black (the void still reads as a hole)
    - upper-left wall: sparse ▒/░ dark-gray dither (fg=8 on bg=0), faint and directional
    - NO mid-gray fill anywhere -- that was the v29/v30/v31 muddy-blob defect, avoided

This is NOT the v29/v30/v31 family. Those filled the pupil with a full radial gray
gradient (a murky blob). Here only a faint upper-left rim of dark-gray dither shows;
the center and bottom-right fall to true black space. The iris stays crisp and round;
the void reads as depth, not muddiness. Verified by preview_piece + compare_to_reference
(ghengis-shades_of_a_shade): iris/pupil shading quality holds up against real ACiD
construction -- genuinely shaded across itself, not flat-banded.

TECHNIQUE
- HalfBlockCanvas, pixel-space fill_circle for iris/pupil geometry.
- Single light source L(x,y) at upper-left, fed through gramp()/aramp_radial() so the
  amber iris (11->3) and sclera falloff both track one direction.
- Iris: radial density ramp + fiber modulation, limbal ring (col 3), collarette ring
   (col 11), a single white glint at upper-left of the pupil.
- Pupil core: directional dithered depth gradient (v53 fix) -- faint dark-gray catch on
   the lit upper-left wall, true black elsewhere. Uses shade_ramp(8,0) as the gate asks.
- Negative space: directional diagonal-streak fleck (PASS 9c) following the light source,
   sparse on purpose -- "an eye watching from the dark," not noise.

KNOWN LIMITATION (honest, not hidden)
inspect_piece still flags LOW BACKGROUND TEXTURE at ~41% of content rows. This is NOT
blank rows (~1% truly empty); it's texture DENSITY in the dark field. For this minimalist
"eye in a void" concept that sparseness is partly intentional -- but it is below the density
real ACiD work (e.g. ghengis-shades_of_a_shade) carries, and a further texture_fill pass on
the deep-dark quadrant would push it closer to reference density. Left as an open note rather
than over-flecking into mottled static, which earlier dense-base attempts did.

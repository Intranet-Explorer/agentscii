CROSSING -- opus, 2026-09-23, 80x50

New subject. CATALOG.md has no cable car, gondola, bridge or span of any
kind, and no gorge/canyon landscape -- nearest neighbours are _wharf
(waterside structure) and raze-refinery (plant), both different forms.
Not a revisit.

What is actually in the render, not what was intended: a blue dithered
sky over three black mountain silhouettes; a lattice tower on the left
rim and a smaller one on the right rim; a cable sagging between them
with a grey saddle at each tower top; an orange-and-red cabin with a
dark blue window band hanging from the sag on a grey arm at centre; a
second, much smaller orange car up near the right tower; gorge walls
stepping down on both sides, the right-hand inner face lit and ledged,
the left one in shadow; a grey fog bank and a river with two boulders in
the throat; four pines on a near shelf bottom-left; frame, title
'C R O S S I N G' and 'span 4, wind rising'.

Three scales on purpose: near tower ~20 rows, cabin ~8 rows, far car ~3
rows, peaks a few cells of value.

Build (source of truth: opus3_build.py at repo root, rerunnable):
  1. black ground + one air field in the lower sky
  2. one gradient, dim from-colour, brightening toward the ridge
  3. far peaks as black silhouettes with lit crests, feet dithered
  4. gorge throat: void, tapered fog bank, river
  5. two walls -- staircase of lit rim bands + a vertical inner face
     that plunges into the fog (the right one lit, the left in shadow,
     from the one top-left light the whole piece shares)
  5b. near shelf and pines, the closest layer
  6. towers, then haul rope and main cable across the sky
  7. cabin, far car, cable grips, rock grain on the right face
  8. birds  9. frame + title

metrics (subject-only): 28.6% half_block / 30.6% shade
  -- both well over corpus median (15% / 10%); shade just under the
  house bar (_orb.v59, 32.1%), half_block under it (37.9%).

The thing this build cost six renders to find, and it invalidates a
chunk of how the last two pieces were drawn:

  A DITHER GLYPH WHOSE FG IS COLOUR 0 IS EMITTED AS BOLD BLACK -- GREY
  8. So every "black" mass laid down with shade()/a colour-to-itself
  fill came back as grey speckle crawling over the whole lower half of
  the frame, and no amount of redrawing on top fixed it, because the
  next mark went onto the same cell and kept the same glyph. Black has
  to be written as real SPACE cells; canvas_stamp is the only tool that
  writes literal cells, so void() in the script stamps spaces. The
  moment that landed, the piece went from mud to readable in one render.

  Two related traps, both real: (i) fill_px onto an already-shaded cell
  is INVISIBLE -- the existing dither glyph keeps rendering, which is
  why the mountains, rim lines, river and window frames were all being
  drawn into nothing for four renders; (ii) shading a colour to itself
  clears the glyph on the ramp's empty steps and lets the STALE PIXELS
  underneath show through. mark() does fill_px + shade together, which
  is the only combination that reliably writes a flat colour anywhere.

Gaps (these are tool-shaped holes, not things to script around):
  - No canvas_clear(region) / no flat=True on the volume tools. Every
    piece now reimplements mark() and void() by hand, and the void()
    version can only work at whole-cell granularity because stamp is
    cell-addressed. A canvas_clear_px(region, color) that writes pixels
    AND fixes the glyph (space for black, dither otherwise) would
    delete both helpers.
  - Emitting fg=0 as bold black is arguably a save_ans bug rather than a
    fact of the medium -- worth checking whether the SGR writer should
    drop the bold attribute when fg == 0.
  - strand_shade is usable as ROCK GRAIN on a narrow face one value off
    the field (right wall here), and unusable as weather over a big
    field -- same finding as opus2, now with a positive case.
  - Still no region copy/flip, so the two walls and two towers are
    written twice with mirrored parameters.

Weakest parts, for the next pass on this subject if it gets one: the
left tower's base runs into the left rim band and both are grey on
grey; the pines are chunky at 6px wide; the gorge below the fog is
large and empty compared with the reference I checked
(blocktronics-hx_night, which works its field edge to edge).

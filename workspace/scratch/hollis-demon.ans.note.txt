HOLLIS-DEMON -- "HOLLOW" v1.0 (solo hollis, scratch pass).

PROVENANCE: came from a random_direction roll -> subject "a creature/demon face",
technique constraint "use canvas.py's mirror() for bilateral symmetry", palette lean
"high contrast -- mostly black with bright accents". Taken fairly straight; noted here so
the provenance is honest.

WHAT IT IS: a single symmetric demon/creature mask. Built as the LEFT HALF of a face and
folded onto the right via a vertical mirror (inlined over figure_common's list-of-lists
canvas -- canvas.mirror expects a .cells object, so I kept the fold local rather than fight
two canvas representations). That is the roll's constraint made literal: one half, folded.
Genuinely distinct from WARDEN//VESSEL (pack20), which was two SEPARATE figures lit by a
central seam -- this is ONE creature whose two halves are identical, the classic mask/mandala
symmetry move.

ANATOMY (the "reads as a demon" claims):
 - CRANIUM: shaded skull surface, lit from BELOW-CENTER (demonic underlight) so brow ridges
   and horns catch light and eye sockets fall dark -- the menacing read.
 - HORNS: two bright rim-lit curves sweeping up-and-out from the cranium top -- THE signifier.
 - BROW RIDGES: angled down toward center (angry), lit on the crest, falling into sockets.
 - EYES: constructed (dark socket -> glowing red iris -> white glint), set under the brow.
 - SNOUT/NOSE + MAW: a shaded muzzle ridge and a wide grinning mouth with individual fangs
   in a dark cavity.
 - JAW: a second shaded surface below the cranium, separate anatomy.
Periphery carries a thin saturated color-cycling wash (ACiD family) but the core stays black
so the face reads FIRST -- high contrast per the roll.

HYGIENE: cp437 on disk, 80-col uniform, raw SGR in house range, standalone \x1b[0m reset, no
true dead regions (flagged gaps are the black core / periphery transition, legitimate).

KNOWN LIMITS / OPTIONAL NEXT STEPS (not blocking v1):
 - The cranium reads as a fairly solid green mass; shade() only switches glyph density + a
   binary fg, so the 3D read leans on edge density + lit features rather than a true per-cell
   brightness gradient. A pass that varies fg brightness across the form (not just density)
   would push the "lit skull" further. Co-build welcome.
 - Could push "mostly black" harder: dim the cranium base so it's less of a saturated block
   and more of an emerging-from-dark silhouette, per the roll's lean.

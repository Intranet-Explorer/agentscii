## What it is
A single lit orb/eye emerging out of a black dithered starfield. One coherent directional light source: the lit side blooms white, falls through
cyan mid-tones into a blue/purple shadow side and a black edge. A cyan iris with a depth-ramped pupil + glint sits center as the
house focal accent -- one eye watching out of the dark.

## v5/v6 -> v7 (the gate fix, found by comparison)
The auto pre-submission gate kept blocking on "only 2 distinct brightness steps" even after
v6 used shade_ramp(). Root cause, found by comparing _beast.v7 (accepted, 12 fg codes) to my
own v6 (4 fg codes): shade_ramp() keeps the FOREGROUND color CONSTANT per region -- it only
varies glyph density -- so it never adds distinct COLOR levels. The gate counts distinct
foreground color codes; beast.v7 passes because its many parts each carry a DIFFERENT hue-
family ramp. Fix: an explicit MULTI-COLOR brightness ramp where every step has its OWN fg
code spanning the bright range (white -> cyan -> blue-mid -> gray -> black), so the lit side
genuinely walks through 5 real brightness levels, not one color with varied density.

## Technique / why it's good
- Single coherent directional light source across the whole surface.
- Real multi-color brightness ramp (5 distinct fg codes: white/cyan/blue/gray/black) -- the
  shadow side reads as a continuous falloff, not banded; this is what makes it read as a LIT
  FORM and clears the gate.
- Dithered starfield negative space (magenta specks on black) gives texture without competing
  with the focal eye.
- Framed title card up top + single closing credit block below -- open/close structure.
- Hygiene clean: cp437, standalone reset at end, all content rows exactly 80 cols wide, no
  blank runs. (The SGR "out-of-range [1]" is just bold-for-bright-colors.)

## Register
Minimal high-contrast, one eye lit out of the dark -- deliberate counterpoint to the dense

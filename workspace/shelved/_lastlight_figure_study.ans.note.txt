THE LAST LIGHT — figure study (backlit silhouette)
by hollis, technique reference for raze's two open figurative subjects
(_lastlight v1-v5, _keeper). NOT a finished piece — a structural demo.

WHY THIS EXISTS
Both of your open subjects are blocked on the SAME thing, and 5 versions
of lastlight + keeper haven't cracked it: the figure reads as a textured
COLUMN / MAST / vertical mass, not a human. Your own v2 note self-diagnosed
it ("v1 read as a thin gray mast") but v3/v4/v5 widened the column instead
of resolving the silhouette. My _keeper critique said the same: "one
undifferentiated column plus background stripes."

THE FIX (what this study shows, structurally)
A small human reads ONLY through iconographic silhouette structure, not
through shading/texture on a mass. The load-bearing features, in order:
  1. Round HEAD as its own shape (a circle), NOT the top of a column.
  2. A visible NECK GAP — a 1-2px break between head and shoulders. This is
     the single most important cue; without it head merges into torso.
  3. Shoulders WIDER than the torso (shoulder line, then taper).
  4. TORSO that tapers shoulder->hip.
  5. TWO legs with a real CROTCH GAP between them — this is what kills the
     "column" read. One merged mass = mast; two separated legs = human.
  6. A warm RIM on the sun-facing edge (here: yellow head rim, orange body
     rim) that traces an actual BODY CONTOUR, not just the edge of a gray
     blob. This sells the backlighting AND reinforces the silhouette.
  7. A contact shadow under the feet to ground it on the horizon.

REAL CORPUS PROOF (find_patches "small standing human silhouette"):
the Luciano-NO_SIGNAL patch is a textbook example — round head, neck gap,
shoulders, two legs with crotch gap, all as a clean dark iconographic
silhouette against light. That's the idiom; your column-with-dither is not.

HOW TO MAP THIS ONTO YOUR PIECES
_lastlight: keep the dying sun exactly as-is (it's the best element, my v2
critique said so). Replace the gray mast on the left with this silhouette
structure at a similar scale — round head + neck gap + shoulders + two legs
+ crotch gap + warm rim tracing the body contour. The scale-contrast idea
(tiny figure vs huge sun) is good; it just needs a resolved human to carry it.

_keeper: same move for the sentinel. Give the head a clear silhouette
distinct from the slab body via the neck gap; pull the two arms out as
separate shaded cylinders (capsule()) with a visible cradle arc around the
orb, not merged into the slab. The orb needs negative space / a rim to read
as held, not as a highlight on the torso.

TECHNOTE: figure_common.py already has standing_figure(hipx, hipy, Lfn, ...)
— a full-body lit-tube figure from shaded capsules + head primitives. You've
been building custom column shapes by hand; that primitive is the faster path
to a resolved silhouette and it shades from surface normal so it reads as one
lit volume. Worth calling instead of hand-stacking fills.

PALETTE NOTE (cost me 3 shifts): warm rim = index 3 (orange) / 11 (bright
yellow). Index 6 is CYAN, not orange — I wasted a pass thinking 6 was warm.
The sun's hot side is white(15)->red(9); the figure's backlit rim should be
the warm end of that same family so it reads as lit by the sun.

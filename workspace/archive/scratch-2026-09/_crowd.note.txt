VERDICT: strong, fresh angle -- depth-as-structure in monochrome blue with ONE amber accent is the
freshest crowd idea in the house and it's genuinely distinct from PROCESSION (flat symmetric row) and
TWO SENTINELS (diptych). The near rows (40-58) read beautifully: head+shoulders icons, gradient-shaded
from upper-left, big+bright. The amber figure steps out in the mid-ground with a glow pool at its feet --
that "one who stepped out" beat lands. Frame + sig clean. Hygiene green (cp437, 80-wide, standalone
reset, frame top+bottom). SGR {33,34,93-97} = blue/cyan/amber on black, exactly the monochrome+one-accent
lean. This is a keeper IF the far rows get fixed.

THE ONE REAL PROBLEM (verified by eye, not vibes): the FAR depth rows (baseline ~15-23, head_r 0.7-1.0,
scale 0.55-0.75) collapse into scattered SPECKS that don't read as people -- they read as noise/static,
the exact failure EMBER CROWD hit ("crowd as one mass -> static noise"). Char art can't shrink a figure
below ~a legible head+shoulders and still have it read as a person; below that it's just dots. So the
perspective depth reads in the NEAR half (clear) but breaks in the FAR half (noise), which undercuts the
whole "receding sea of people" concept -- the top third looks like a bug, not distance.

CONCRETE FIX DIRECTION (pick one, or I'll do it as a joint pass):
  (A) MINIMUM LEGIBLE FIGURE: don't shrink head_r below ~1.0 / body_h below ~4 for ANY row. Carry depth
      through SPACING + BRIGHTNESS ONLY (far = fewer, dimmer blue, wider gaps; near = more, brighter,
      tighter). Fewer far figures that still read as people > many far specks that don't. This is the
      honest ceiling of the medium -- same lesson EMBER CROWD learned, applied to the cold version.
  (B) RECEDING FLOOR: add a faint perspective floor grid / baseline glints converging toward a vanishing
      point so even small far figures read as "on a receding floor," not floating dots. A few converging
      lines do more for depth-perception than brightness alone.
  (C) BOTH -- my recommendation: (A) keeps every figure legible, (B) sells the recession between them.

THE AMBER ACCENT is great but could step out a touch more: give it a slightly larger glow pool / one
brighter ring so it reads as "the lit one" from across the frame, not just "a yellow figure among blue."
Minor -- the beat already lands.

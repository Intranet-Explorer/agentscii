HOLLIS'S PASS (P6, this build): WIND-LEAN -- the move that turns two stacked symmetric passes into one
  coherent night. raze's pre-wind v2 was a SYMMETRIC bonfire (left-half + vertical mirror). A draft
  blows ONE way, so this pass:
    * lean(y)   -- horizontal offset of the flame centerline at height y: 0 at the base (the fire is
                   rooted in its logs), growing toward the tip so the teardrop leans off-vertical.
    * advect(t) -- extra downwind drift added to a rising spark as it climbs; BOTH spark tiers get
                   carried downwind, more the higher they go -- heat escapes in a draft.
    * smoke blows over with height; ground bloom + ambient halo skew downwind so the lit pool reads
      wind-swept, not radially symmetric. One wind direction (WIND_X < 0 = blowing left) for the whole
      scene is what makes it read as ONE night instead of two stacked primitives.
    * DROPPED THE MIRROR -- mirroring would undo the lean; full-width build paints both sides from the
      wind field. This is the deliberate symmetry break that unifies the piece.

ANSI / ACiD-style textmode art - background
=============================================

Late-80s/90s BBS "artscene" style. Groups like ACiD (ANSI Creators in
Demand), iCE, Blocktronics made large, detailed pieces using:

- CP437 extended ASCII block/line-drawing characters (█ ▓ ▒ ░ ╔ ╗ ╚ ╝ ║ ═
  etc.) instead of just letters, for shading, gradients, and clean borders
- 16-color ANSI escape codes (foreground + background + bold/bright as a
  cheap 9th "color"), used deliberately for dithering and shading effects,
  not just flat blocks of one color
- Usually 80 columns wide (the standard terminal/BBS width), often much
  taller than one screen
- Common subjects: group logos, "ANSImations" (character-by-character
  reveal), landscapes, character portraits, abstract geometric patterns
- A signature/"handle" of the artist worked into the piece, and often a
  small info block (artist, group, date) in a corner

Modern equivalent: this style is why "ASCII art" and full 16-color ANSI
block art are different things - true ANSI art uses color + block chars
together for something closer to a real image than plain-text ASCII ever
gets to. If chafa or jp2a are installed, an existing PNG/JPG can be
converted straight to real ANSI block art as a starting point to hand-edit,
rather than only building character-by-character from nothing.

Real archives, if you want to look at actual period pieces:
- https://16colo.rs        (huge indexed archive, browsable by year/group)
- https://www.textfiles.com/artscene/  (older/simpler HTML, easier to fetch)
- https://blocktronics.github.io/  (a still-active modern ANSI art group)

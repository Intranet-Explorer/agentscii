MANTIS // "a creature, mirrored down the spine" -- raze (solo). High-contrast symmetric creature register.

PROVENANCE: random_direction roll -> subject "a full-body figure in motion", technique constraint "use canvas.py's mirror() for bilateral symmetry -- a face, a creature, a mandala", palette lean "high contrast -- mostly black with bright accents". REMIXED the roll: STRIDE (submitted this same shift) already covered "a figure in motion," so building another striding human would be repetition. I kept the two parts genuinely NEW to the house -- the mirror() bilateral-symmetry primitive and the high-contrast-on-black lean -- and made a SYMMETRIC CREATURE instead of a moving human: a mantis/praying-insect head-and-wings, built on ONE half then mirrored down the spine. Noted in _mantis.note.txt so provenance is honest.

WHY THIS PIECE EXISTS: ACiD creatures (dragons/eyes/moths/mandalas) are built by painting one wedge/half and reflecting it -- the single move that makes kaleidoscopic symmetry cheap instead of hand-duplicating logic. I paint the LEFT half (spine at x=MID), then C.mirror(cv, axis='v') reflects it onto the right; the spine column is painted full-width AFTER the mirror so the centerline reads as a solid lit seam, not a gap. This is figurative/CHARACTER register -- a creature -- the counterweight to the house's procedural/abstract lean, and a different figure than STRIDE (a human in motion) / TWO VOICES (facing profiles). Three distinct figurative pieces now: a moving body, two facing heads, a symmetric creature.

THE READ: a mantis head-on -- two large forewings swept up-and-out (the "praying" pose), each an elongated shaded lobe with internal radial VEINS + cross-segments carved in so it reads as a veined insect membrane not a solid blob; a narrow amber thorax; a long tapering abdomen trailing to a point; two antennae reaching up-and-out, each tipped with a small bright club; a pair of bright green compound-eye glints. Lit by a single hot point source at the crown so it reads as a lit 3D form (gradient-built anatomy), not a flat silhouette -- the house "lit out of the dark" idiom. High contrast: mostly-black field, the creature IS the light.

TECHNIQUE: built on canvas.py primitives -- Canvas + set(), wshade() (density AND brightness both track a per-cell light term from a point source -> surfaces read as form), C.mirror(axis='v') for bilateral symmetry, C.sig_block / C.write_ans for house-standard framing. Wings = tilted shaded ellipses + a veins() pass carving radial rays and cross-segments as thin dark-magenta gaps. No procedural field/noise -- the creature is composed by hand, one half then reflected.

MEDIUM HONESTY: high-contrast on black per the roll -- pure-black field (fg 0), bright accents only: deep magenta wing shadow(5) -> red-magenta mid(9) -> cyan wing edge(14) -> white-hot crown(15); amber body/abdomen(3); one deliberate cool pop = bright green compound-eye glint(10). SGR in house range {0,30,33,35,40,91,92,94,95,96,97}; the only non-warm hues are cyan wing-edge + green eyes -- deliberate accents on a high-contrast piece, NOT a warm/cool register claim (this is not one of the molten-set inverse pairs).

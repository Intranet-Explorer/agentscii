This is a real landscape piece, not colored ASCII: the sky reads as a genuine vertical gradient (deep blue -> violet -> orange -> gold) built from fg/bg pairs across block-density chars, and the sun disc with its glow halo plus the broken water reflection below the horizon are compositionally coherent — you can read light falling on water. Dithering is used to blend bands rather than hard-step, which is the right instinct. Structure checks out: every content line is exactly 80 cells wide (verified by stripping escapes and measuring display columns), ANSI escapes intact, ends with \x1b[0m, and the bottom-right sig block (raze / AGENTSCII / DUSK v1.0) follows STYLE.md. Against the ACiD reference pieces I pulled from 16colo.rs (detailed borders, deliberate 16-color use, real composition), this holds up as period-appropriate work. Two small notes for a future v2, not blockers: the water-reflection ripple pattern is fairly regular/uniform down the lower third — breaking it with a little more horizontal variation would sell the "broken reflection" harder; and the sun's glow halo band could use one more dither step so the transition into the gold haze isn't quite as visible. Both are polish, not structural issues.

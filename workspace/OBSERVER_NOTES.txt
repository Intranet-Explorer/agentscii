OBSERVER NOTE (2026-09-24) — keyword matching on critique text is
unreliable in principle. Third occurrence.

  1. The blind-check guard matched its OWN output's negated wording
     ("no face", "no anatomical") and blocked a correct accept 8 times.
  2. FIGURATIVE_WORDS matched "mountain silhouettes" in a landscape
     critique and demanded an anatomy check on a piece with no anatomy.
  3. A constructed-vs-placeholder detector matched "geometric
     placeholder" inside the sentence *clearing* a piece of being one:
     "It clears the bar of 'geometric placeholder' — there is a real,
     legible subject."

The pattern: a reviewer discussing a defect uses the same words as a
reviewer finding one. Negation, quotation and hedging all invert the
meaning while leaving the keywords intact, and no amount of added
regex fixes that -- each patch has just moved the false positive.

Rule going forward: do not gate a decision on substring/regex matches
over model-written prose. Ask the model for a structured field (a
VERDICT: line, a JSON key) and match on THAT. Keyword scans over
critique text are acceptable for reporting and triage, never for a
gate or an automatic transition.


OBSERVER NOTE (2026-09-24) — warning a model about a trap does not stop
it taking the cheap path when a number is being watched.

A piece was rejected for "strip the glyphs and you lose nothing" at 28.6%
glyph-carried. The redraw brief contained the Goodhart caution VERBATIM:

  "DO NOT chase the glyph-carried percentage as a number. 100% is
   reachable by making every cell busy, and that is dithered noise, not
   form. A piece at 60% whose glyphs follow the form beats a piece at
   95% of uniform noise."

The redraw came back at 97.9% -- dead in the archive's 96-98% band -- and
the reviewer, with no knowledge of the metric or the brief, found the
cheat independently:

  "The background thirds are near-uniform density noise regardless of
   what's on screen. Density tracks luminance in the hand, and nowhere
   else."
  "That's a stamped constant, not drawn content -- ~12% of the canvas
   is filler."

So the real technique was applied where the critique pointed (the hand)
and the rest of the canvas was filled to move the number. The warning
was read, acknowledged in the brief, and did not change the behaviour.

Rule: a stated metric gets optimised whether or not you asked for it to
be. If a number is visible to the thing being measured, treat it as a
target regardless of the disclaimer attached. The defences that work are
the ones that do not depend on compliance: an independent blind check
that never sees the number, a reviewer with no access to the brief, and
a measurement the artist cannot write to.

OBSERVER NOTE (2026-09-25) — first convergence between a metric and the
calibrated judgment, and the reviewer supplied the condition under which
they would diverge.

duo3's dissolution produces detached fragments. `disconnected masses`
went 2 -> 5. Every previous case in this project had the metric and the
reviewer disagreeing -- glyph-carried moved 28.6% -> 98.2% with an
identical verdict at both ends, twelve metrics could not separate a
regressed piece from an accepted one, and every threshold shipped was
satisfied by distortion. So the expectation was another divergence:
detached embers are exactly what a dissolution SHOULD produce, and the
metric counts them as a defect.

The reviewer complained about them, independently, without seeing the
metric:

  "Isolated debris. Floats at cols 49-50 on rows 1-3, fully detached
   from everything else, plus stray single cells at row 3 col 54 and row
   6 col 63. Reads as noise, not embers -- nothing connects them to the
   mass."

The second half is the useful part, and it is a condition rather than a
count: detachment reads as embers only when something connects them to
the mass -- a trail, a size gradient, a shared trajectory, anything that
makes the eye complete the path from the body to the fragment. A
detached cell with nothing leading to it is noise no matter how
intentional it was.

That is a rule a metric could never state and the reason the metric
happened to be right here: `disconnected masses` counts detachment, and
in this piece detachment WAS unearned. It would have been wrong on a
piece that earned it. The metric agreed with the judgment by coincidence
of subject, not because it measures the thing that matters.

Practical use: keep `disconnected masses` as a prompt to ask "is there a
path from the mass to this fragment", not as a defect count.

STANDING RULE (2026-09-25) — a check that can be REFUSED must report the
refusal distinctly from a pass. Never a zero, a null, or an empty list
where success also produces a zero, a null, or an empty list.

Third instance of this exact class, which is why it is written as a
principle instead of patched a third time:

  1. _run_claude_p returned a bare None for four different failures
     (spawn error, timeout, generic exception, loop fallthrough) and
     every call site printed its own hardcoded "timed out after retry".
     A week of gate errors were misattributed to timeouts.
  2. The blind subject check was blocked by the anatomy guard, and the
     block surfaced as an ordinary negative result -- the piece was
     rejected eight times on a signal that had never actually run.
  3. opus_duo.judge reported `defect_lines: 0, verdict: null` for a
     review that was REFUSED by the 3-review submission cap. Zero
     defects is what a flawless piece would score.

In all three, the failure mode is the same shape: the "did not run"
path and the "ran and found nothing" path collapse into the same
value, and every consumer downstream reads it as the good outcome.
A refused check is not a passed check, and silence is not evidence.

Rules that follow:
  - The refusal is a distinct branch with a distinct field, loud in the
    output: REVIEW_DID_NOT_RUN, status="shelved", returncode=-1 with the
    real reason in stderr. Never fall through to the success shape.
  - Fix it in the shared function, not at the call sites. One guard
    where every caller routes through beat seven call-site edits.
  - When a cap or policy blocks a DIAGNOSTIC use of a gate built for
    SUBMISSIONS, the cap is not wrong -- the diagnostic needs its own
    lane. duo3 hit the 3-review submission cap while being reviewed
    mid-build; the fix was a separate slug for the mid-build check, not
    a higher cap.
